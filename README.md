# CS255
Q:  Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?  
A:  The DriverPass project was made for Liam, the owner of DriverPass, with the goal of improving driver training to reduce high DMV driving test failure rates. The system provides online courses, practice tests, and scheduled live road tests accessible via web browsers and mobile devices. It includes various system components such as account management, data tracking, comprehensive reporting, direct DMV updates integration, and strong security measures to ensure reliable performance.

Q:  What did you do particularly well?  
A:  While I never used Lucidcharts before, I felt the UML Diagrams were done particularly well. I have some experience using various MS Office programs as well as paint, gimp and photoshop so I was no stranger to the layout of Lucidcharts. This experience aided in completing the diagrams fairly quickly.

Q:  If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?  
A:  I would revise the entire business requirements document. I was procrastinating far too much that week and decided to cliff note a good portion of the document. I would go back and include  more details regarding functional and non-functional requirements and provide more insight to security features.

Q:  How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?  
A:  The user's needs were interpreted through a clear understanding of the primary issues, which were high DMV failure rates and insufficient training support. These were directly translated into system features such as progress tracking, detailed user data management, integrated DMV updates, and multi-platform compatibility. Considering user needs is important as it ensures the final system effectively addresses actual problems users face. This increases the system's functionality and overall user satisfaction.

Q:  How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?  
A:  The design approach involved defining clear system objectives, identifying specific user requirements, and translating these into structured UML diagrams. In the future, I would include strategies that improve user stories, provide more rigorous scenario testing, and install iterative user feedback loops early in the design phase to validate requirements and usabilit. Also, applying Agile and Scrum methodologies would be important to adapt to changing needs more effectively and to ensure the client's needs are addressed in a timely matter.
